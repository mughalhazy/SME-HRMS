01 — Foundations
Design Principles
Six principles guide every decision in Meridian — from pixel-level typography to macro layout choices.
🎯
Data First
HR software surfaces dense, critical information. Every layout prioritises data legibility over decoration. Numbers use monospace. Tables stay scannable. Colour encodes meaning, never decoration.
⚡
Zero Friction
Approvals, form submissions, and data lookups are daily-use workflows. Interactions should require the fewest possible clicks and never leave users guessing what comes next.
🛡️
Trust Through Clarity
Payroll, compliance, and employment records carry legal weight. Visual hierarchy and semantic colour create immediate confidence that the right information is being shown.
🤖
AI as Co-pilot
AI features are surfaced with teal AI cards, confidence scores, and pulse animations. AI assists but never replaces human judgement — it always surfaces confidence and explains its reasoning.
📐
Consistent Density
Enterprise users spend hours inside the product. Padding is tight but never cramped — 4px grid, single-line table rows, and concise chip labels keep information density high without fatigue.
🌱
Progressive Complexity
Simple views stay simple. Advanced features (builder, analytics deep-dives) reveal complexity progressively. The sidebar nav scales from icon-only (collapsed) to full labels without layout shift.

Rules
Always use JetBrains Mono for any number that will be compared, sorted, or scanned — salaries, counts, IDs, timestamps, percentages.
Never mix font weights within a single logical element. A card title is always 700. A row value is always 400–500.
Uppercase labels use 9.5–10px Inter 700, letter-spacing 0.10em, color --t4. Never larger, never bolder.
Negative letter-spacing (−0.02 to −0.04em) is reserved for display sizes only — 16px and above.

Motion is purposeful and brief. It communicates state change and directs attention — never entertains. All transitions respect reduced-motion preferences.

+60ms per element

800–900ms · cubic-bezier(.25,.8,.25,1)

2s · infinite

AI Components
AI surfaces are always visually distinct — teal or amber, with a pulsing dot, confidence score, and "AI" label. Users should always know when they're looking at an AI finding vs. factual data.

AI — Attrition Risk
Moderate flight risk identified
Engagement score dropped 18 pts over 14 days. Pattern matches pre-departure behaviour in similar profiles.

Always show confidence % — users need to calibrate trust. Displayed as a labelled progress bar, not just a number.
Pulsing dot signals live/active AI monitoring. Static dot = completed analysis. Never pulse on resolved states.
Teal = informational AI (dashboard bar, suggestions). Amber = actionable warning (attrition risk, anomaly). Never red for AI — that colour is reserved for system errors.
Always dismissable. Users who disagree or have already taken action should be able to remove AI cards from their view.

17 — Patterns
Data Visualisation
Charts use SVG or CSS — no external chart libraries required. A strict colour order applies. Donut charts always show a centre label. All charts animate on first load.
Chart colour order

#F59E0BMarketing / Fourth series

#EF4444Finance / Sixth series
#64748BOther / Neutral
Chart rules
Trend lines use --teal with 8% fill opacity below the line. Current value point shows teal circle with pulsing ring.
Bar charts in dashboard use grouped bars — each department gets its fixed colour, always in the same order.
Donut charts use SVG stroke-dasharray. Track stroke: #F1F4F8. Centre always shows the total in JetBrains Mono bold.
Inline bar tracks in tables: height 5px, background --t5, border-radius 99px. Colour matches the department series colour.
All charts animate on first render — bars and fills start at 0 and animate to their value with cubic-bezier(.25,.8,.25,1).
18 — Patterns
Voice & Tone
Meridian communicates with confidence and brevity. It surfaces facts, not opinion — and when AI speaks, it qualifies with confidence scores, not absolutes.
Labels & Actions
Lead with verbs: "Add Employee", "Approve", "Export CSV"
Table headers are nouns only: "Department", "Status", "Salary"
Nav items are nouns: "Employees", "Analytics", "Settings"
Error messages say what happened and what to do: "SLA breached (5d) — approve now"
AI Language
Prefix all AI findings: "AI —" followed by the finding type
State confidence explicitly: "83% confidence", never just "high confidence"
Plain language: "3 employees show elevated attrition signals" not "attrition likelihood vectors elevated"
Always recommend an action: "Recommend scheduling a check-in"
Numbers & Data
Salary: always $185,000 — no rounding unless explicitly a summary
Dates: Mar 10, 2026 in UI · 2026-03-10 in export data
Deltas: +14 / −3 / ±0 — always with sign prefix
Employee IDs: EMP-0341 — always 4-digit zero-padded, monospace
✓ Do — Meridian voice
"3 records deviate >15% from last cycle. Review before April 1 run."
Direct. Specific. Time-bounded. Tells user exactly what to do.
✕ Don't — Generic voice
"Some payroll records may require your attention."
Vague. No numbers. No urgency. No action. Users ignore it.
